# Ramadan Planner: competitor and market analysis

**Date:** 2026-11-03 · **Scope:** a Bengali-first Ramadan companion (PWA) for Bangladesh and the Bengali diaspora · **Launch target:** Ramadan 1448 (Feb 2027) · **Model:** mostly free, funded by donations and community collaboration

**How the numbers were checked.** Google Play figures (install bucket, Play's raw install count, rating, ad and IAP flags, last-updated date) were pulled from the Google Play listing for country=BD on 2026-11-03, using the public listing page and the `google-play-scraper` library, which reads the same page. Play's raw install count appears as "raw" below; the public bucket (for example "5M+") is the official figure. Review themes come from the most recent 300–800 reviews per app, filtered to 1–2 stars. Anything I could not confirm is marked **unverified**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **The Bengali Islamic-app market is crowded and mature, but no one owns a dedicated Ramadan planner.** All-in-one apps lead it: [Muslim Bangla](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tos.namajtime) (5M+, ads plus IAP) and [Muslims Day by Kahf](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=theoaktroop.appoframadan) (5M+, "halal ads" plus premium). Ad-free reference apps from nonprofits are the other strong group: Greentech (UK charity) and IRD Foundation. The Ramadan-specific planners on Play BD are small: [Ramadan Planner by Shohoj Islam](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.assunnahfoundation.ramadanplanner) has 10K+ installs and rates 4.2, held down by registration and OTP failures. [রামাদান প্ল্যানার by GearUp Lab](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gearuplab.ramadanplanner) has 10K+ installs and has not been updated since 2021.
- **The founder's attribution claim is wrong.** "Al Hadith / iHadis" comes from **IRD Foundation** (Bangladesh), not Greentech. Greentech's flagship is **Al Quran (Tafsir & by Word)**, with 10M+ installs (raw about 14.7M). Its Bangla prayer app is **Sadiq**, with 500K+. Islami Jindegi is at **1M+**, not 500K+. Muslim Bangla's 5M+ is confirmed.
- **A donation-funded, ad-free model is proven in this niche.** The clearest benchmark is Greentech: its income grew from about £56K (2020) to about £248K (2024), it reports 7.5M+ users, and it frames giving as sadaqah jariyah plus a Waqf Fund. Quran.com (a 501(c)(3), framed as a "waqf") and Hadith BD (personal bKash, Rocket and Nagad donations) show the same model at very different scales. There is a warning sign: a Greentech user dropped a review from 5★ to 1★ over "constant pop-ups asking for donations". Donation asks must stay quiet.
- **The most common user complaints are ads (Muslim Pro, ad-supported Ramadan calendars), prayer alarms that do not fire on time, wrong Hijri dates and moon-sighting mismatches, forced updates and online-only regressions, and forced login.** Ramadan Planner can win on all five: an offline-first PWA, no ads, no account needed, calendars that follow Islamic Foundation Bangladesh's moon sighting, and reliable reminders.
- **Ramadan 1448 is expected to start on Mon 8 Feb 2027 by the Gulf/astronomical estimate, and most likely on Tue 9 Feb in Bangladesh**, which usually follows Saudi Arabia by a day. That leaves about 14 weeks from today. In 2026, reviews of BD Ramadan apps clustered on 18–28 Feb, so most installs happen in the first days of Ramadan. The product should be live and indexed by mid-January, before Shab-e-Barat (around 23–24 Jan 2027).

## 3. Deep-dives

**Muslim Bangla (TopOfStack).** Confirmed: 5M+ installs and ad-supported with IAP. The iOS IAPs are ad removals, not a subscription, which suggests the model is "pay to remove ads" plus commerce. Confirmed features: matrimony ("Bibaho", at marriage.muslimbangla.com; the site's "বিবাহ" nav link points to **muslimakd.com**, so "Muslim Akd" is plausibly the matrimony brand, but who runs it is **unverified**), a halal marketplace, a quiz with prizes ([iOS listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id878084956); the site says prizes are given "often"), and "voice calls with a Mufti". **Whether the mufti call is paid is unverified.** One recent review says the app "asks for money for all tasks", and another calls the matrimony section a "ডাকাত সেকশন" (robber section). That is the reputational risk of this model. Main takeaway: it is a super-app, and a focused Ramadan tool should not try to beat it on breadth.

**Muslims Day (Kahf).** A 5M+ app that was once "App of Ramadan" and is now year-round. It is the closest large Ramadan-focused competitor. It runs "halal-only" ads through Kahf's own network and sells premium tiers. Complaints include forced updates and premium pricing.

**IRD Foundation (iHadis, Quran Majeed, Dua).** A Bangladeshi nonprofit with an ad-free stack and around 5M raw installs across three apps (Play raw counts). It already publishes **district-level Ramadan calendars** on ihadis.com, which makes it a credible potential content partner as well as a competitor. Its donation page frames giving as sadaqah jariyah. **Its finances are unverified**: I found no public report.

**Greentech Apps Foundation (GTAF).** A UK CIO registered on 4 May 2018, with Bangladesh listed under its geography. Filed income was £56.4K in 2020, £67.5K in 2021, £122.3K in 2022, £152.6K in 2023 and £247.95K in 2024 ([OpenCharities](https://opencharities.uk/ew/1178251)). It is funded by donations (one-off, Donorbox campaigns, and a perpetual **Waqf Fund**) under a "NO ADS" and sadaqah jariyah message ([support page](https://gtaf.org/support/what-do-you-need-to-know-about-gtaf-foundation/), [donate](https://gtaf.org/donate/)). It also earns service income through "Greentech Apps Limited" design and development ([Q3 2023 insights](https://gtaf.org/blog/q3-2023-insights)). For transparency it publishes quarterly "Insights" posts with usage and product updates but no money figures, and it files statutory accounts with the Charity Commission. On UX reputation, its apps rate 4.76–4.83★, and reviews praise the absence of ads. It also publishes a *Ramadan Planner* and *Ramadan Journal* (publication or Notion formats), so the name overlaps with this product.

**Hadith BD.** A personal, non-organisational project started in May 2013. It collects donations through personal bKash, Rocket and Nagad numbers under a "stay with this sadaqah jariyah work" appeal ([donation page](https://www.hadithbd.com/donation/), seen through search; the site blocked automated fetches). Web traffic is **unverified**. It shows that small-scale donation funding with mobile wallets works in BD, at the cost of formal transparency.

**Banglalink Najat.** Telco value-added service: Tk 2 per day for the app/WAP version, IVR from Tk 0.33 per day to Tk 10 per month, plus VAT. **The founder's "2–10 BDT" range is roughly correct** (Tk 2 per day for app/WAP, Tk 10 per month for IVR), but it comes from a search-engine snapshot of Banglalink's page, so treat it as **partly verified**. It is not relevant to a donation model, except as evidence that people will pay small amounts for Islamic content.

**Muslim Pro.** 100M+ on Play and 150M+ downloads worldwide (2023 PR). In the 2020 Motherboard report, Muslim Pro data went to X-Mode, which sold location data to contractors serving the US military. The company called the report "incorrect and untrue" but ended its data partnerships ([MEE](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/us-military-buying-location-data-popular-muslim-app)). The outcome of the French lawsuit is **unverified**. Recent Play reviews are dominated by ad complaints. This is the strongest argument for positioning Ramadan Planner as **no ads, no trackers, no data sales**.

**Quran.com.** Run by a 501(c)(3) (EIN 82-4203288) and called a "waqf … public trust", with free APIs. Third-party data puts 2023 revenue at about $767K (**unverified**). It fiscally sponsors Sunnah.com. This shows that a single US nonprofit can host several open Islamic projects.

**Pillars.** Ad-free and privacy-first (prayer times calculated on the device). Its funding model is **unverified**. Its parent company also builds a "Patron Tool" for mosque funding. It is the design reference for being "privacy-respecting".

**Tarteel and Athan Pro.** Tarteel sells subscriptions and keeps AI features behind a paywall. Athan Pro uses ads plus IAP. Both are paid benchmarks to contrast with.

**Ramadan-specific apps on Play BD.** The two "Ramadan Planner" apps are small (10K+ each). One fails on forced registration; the other was abandoned in 2021. Seasonal calendar apps reach 100K–1M+ installs, which shows there is demand for Sehri/Iftar-by-district content, but they are monetised with ads and complaints follow.

## 4. Gaps and whitespace for Ramadan Planner

1. **No account needed, offline-first.** The only direct namesake (Shohoj Islam) loses users to OTP registration failures. Islami Jindegi, Muslims Day and Sadiq draw complaints about forced updates and lost offline access. A PWA with local storage and optional sync fits this gap.
2. **A trustworthy BD calendar.** Wrong Hijri and Sha'ban dates are common complaints (Muslim Bangla, Islami Jindegi, IUT SIKS). Defaulting to the dates announced by Islamic Foundation Bangladesh and its National Moon Sighting Committee, with a one-tap manual ±1 day adjustment and a visible "source" line, would build trust. The 2026 calendar app praised for "following Islamic Foundation Bangladesh" shows this works.
3. **Reliable reminders.** Alarms not firing is a common complaint. PWA notifications on Android are best-effort, so the product needs a fallback: an ICS calendar export, a "pin to home screen" daily card, and honest guidance on battery-saver settings.
4. **A planner, not a library.** The incumbents are content libraries. A daily amal checklist, a Quran khatam plan, last-10-nights and Laylatul Qadr focus, a family or group plan, and a post-Ramadan "legacy" summary (the Ramadan Legacy concept, now dormant) are not served well in Bangla.
5. **Ad-free and data-minimal by default**, with a public privacy promise. This contrasts with Muslim Pro's history and with the ad-supported BD calendars.
6. **Lightweight.** Muslim Bangla's iOS app is 107 MB. A PWA under about 1–2 MB that works on low-end Android is an advantage (see section 8).
7. **Open content collaboration.** No BD Ramadan product works in the open. Partnering on content (alQuranBD's open API, Quran.com APIs, possibly IRD or Hadith BD with permission) and accepting community translations or reviews would be new for this market.
8. **Naming risk.** "Ramadan Planner" is already used by Shohoj Islam's app, by As-Sunnah Foundation and Shaikh Ahmadullah's book, and by Greentech's publication. Consider a Bangla-distinct sub-brand or a qualifier.

**Legal note (BD).** Under the Foreign Donations (Voluntary Activities) Regulation Act 2016, individuals and NGOs need **prior approval from the NGO Affairs Bureau** to receive foreign donations for voluntary activities, received through a designated "mother account" at a scheduled bank ([ICNL text](https://www.icnl.org/research/library/bangladesh_fdva/)). Get local legal advice before routing diaspora money into Bangladesh. Keeping foreign donations in an entity abroad that pays costs abroad (hosting, domains, foreign-paid contractors) avoids this, but **check it with counsel**.

**Trust norms seen in this space.** Registered-charity numbers in the footer (GTAF), statutory filings (Charity Commission, IRS 990 for Quran Foundation), periodic impact posts (GTAF quarterly insights, which report usage but no money), "waqf" or "public trust" language (Quran.com, GTAF Waqf Fund), and sadaqah jariyah framing (GTAF, IRD, Hadith BD, Quran.com). None of the BD-native projects reviewed publish financials. A simple public **monthly costs-and-donations ledger** would set Ramadan Planner apart.

**Recommendation.**
1. **Launch (Ramadan 2027):** keep every core feature free and free of ads and trackers. Put a quiet "সাদাকায়ে জারিয়া" support page in settings and the footer, plus **one** gentle end-of-Ramadan prompt. No pop-ups during worship flows; GTAF's 1★ donation-popup review is the warning. Use **SSLCommerz or a bKash merchant account** once a trade licence or entity exists. Until then, use a dedicated (non-personal) bKash/Nagad number and a public ledger.
2. **Open-source the code and content pipeline** on GitHub with **Open Collective (OSC)** as the transparent ledger for global and diaspora money, accepting the 10% fee in exchange for zero entity overhead. Spend it outside BD (infrastructure) to stay clear of FD(VA) questions until legal advice is in.
3. **Year 2:** explore fiscal sponsorship or partnership with an established Islamic nonprofit (UK or US) for tax-deductible diaspora giving and Stripe. Publish a yearly impact and finance note.
4. **Avoid:** paywalls, ad networks (including "halal" ads), matrimony or marketplace features, and selling data.

**Launch implications (today is 2026-11-03, about 97 days to 8 Feb):**
- **By mid-Dec 2026:** freeze the MVP scope (calendar, daily checklist, khatam plan, reminders, offline). Content (30 days of ayah, hadith and dua) should be reviewed by a qualified scholar.
- **By mid-Jan 2027:** public beta live and indexed, ahead of Shab-e-Barat (around 23–24 Jan), which is the natural "prepare for Ramadan" moment. Seed partners and communities.
- **Build for date uncertainty:** ship with 9 Feb as the BD default, show an "awaiting moon-sighting" state on 7–8 Feb, and push a config update on the committee's announcement without needing an app update. That is a PWA strength.
- **Install timing:** in 2026, low-star and other reviews for BD Ramadan apps clustered on **18–28 Feb**, around the start of Ramadan. Expect most installs from about 1 day before to day 5 of Ramadan, and make sure servers, caching and CDN can handle that spike.
- The ongoing **Islamic Foundation calendar** dependency means the district Sehri/Iftar table must be ready as soon as IFB publishes it (usually shortly before Ramadan; exact timing **unverified**).

## 8. Device and network context (Bangladesh)

- **Android dominates mobile:** about **91.2% Android, 8.7% iOS** (StatCounter, Aug 2026; 90.7–92.2% across May–Aug 2026). Android 13 is the most common version (17.4%), followed by Android 15 (15.1%) ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/bangladesh), [Android versions](https://gs.statcounter.com/android-version-market-share/mobile-os/bangladesh)). For the diaspora, iOS matters more, so test Safari PWA install and notification limits.
- **Low-end devices are common:** 1Q24 smartphone ASP was **US$128**; the **sub-US$100 segment was 33%** of smartphones; feature phones were **53.9% of all handset shipments** (IDC). Transsion brands (Tecno, itel, Infinix) lead ([IDC](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/press-releases/idc-bangladesh-mobile-phone-market-grew-1-7-in-the-first-quarter-of-2024/)). 5G phones were about 16% of H1 2025 shipments (reported; **unverified primary**).
- **Access is still partial:** **72.4% of households** have at least one smartphone, and **48.9% of individuals aged 5+** used the internet (BBS ICT survey, Jul–Sep 2025). There is a gender gap: 51.2% of men vs 46.3% of women online; 63% vs 53% own a mobile ([Financial Express, Dec 2025](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/mobile-internet-access-expand-despite-ownership-gender-gaps-bbs-survey)). A later quarter reportedly reached 75.1% household smartphone ownership and 59.2% individual internet use (**not independently verified**). This means some households share one phone; consider a family or shared-device mode.
- **Data is cheap per GB but packaged in confusing ways:** cable.co.uk-based estimates range from **about $0.23 to $0.70 per GB** for 2023, depending on the source ([Daily Star](https://images.thedailystar.net/node/3732166), [Dhaka Tribune op-ed](https://www.dhakatribune.com/opinion/op-ed/373659/future-proofing-telecom)). Package prices vary from about ৳16 to ৳143 per GB ([Future Startup, Sep 2025](https://futurestartup.com/2025/09/11/how-complexity-became-telecos-most-profitable-product/)). Users often run on small or validity-limited packs, so a small initial payload, offline caching and no forced online checks all matter.

**Design implications:** a JS budget under about 200 KB gzipped for the first screen; self-hosted, subsetted Bangla font (Noto Sans Bengali or Hind Siliguri, for example) with `font-display: swap`; works offline after the first visit; good performance on 2–3 GB RAM Android Go devices; no login wall.
